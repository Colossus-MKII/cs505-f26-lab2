Metric | CPU (ideal memory) | SoC (implemented memory)
Smallest area | Multi-cycle: one internal bus and a time-shared datapath. | Multi-cycle: it can also share one RAM access port between instructions and data.
Fewest cycles | Single-cycle: one instruction per active clock, including dependent instructions. | Single-cycle: the on-chip RAM is combinational on reads and does not add a clocked wait.
Shortest period | Multi-cycle: its controller splits instruction processing into small steps. | Multi-cycle: one RAM lookup per step avoids the single-cycle fetch-to-data-read chain.
Fastest low-ilp | Pipeline: ALU forwarding should sustain adjacent dependent arithmetic at a shorter period than single-cycle. | Pipeline: the dependency still forwards and fetch/data logic is separated by pipeline registers.
Fastest high-ilp | Pipeline: several independent operations can occupy different stages simultaneously. | Pipeline: independent arithmetic leaves the data memory idle, and sequential fetches can overlap execution.
Fastest suite overall | Pipeline: the shorter period should outweigh fill, flush and load delays across the suite. | Pipeline: dividing the SoC path should save more time than control hazards cost on average.
Lowest energy across suite | Single-cycle: fewest clock edges and no interstage registers. | Pipeline: a large RAM and its clocked cells contribute every cycle, but the shorter execution time may limit leakage enough to offset stalls and register overhead.
Lowest power across suite | Multi-cycle: only a portion of the shared datapath needs to switch in each step, although a high operating frequency could reverse this. | Multi-cycle: a single RAM port and one memory operation per step should reduce switched capacitance.
For energy and power, compare geometric means across the same eleven workloads, each design at its own critical-path frequency. The latency prediction uses the same suite weighting as the requested normalized comparisons.
